We overestimate what we know.

Not occasionally. Not in moments of arrogance or stress. Consistently, reliably, and quietly — across cultures, professions, and levels of education. This is not a character flaw. It is a feature of the human mind, built in over millennia of evolution that rewarded fast decisions far more than accurate ones.

The problem is that the world we live in now rewards accuracy. And the cost of certainty — the cost of being more confident than the evidence warrants — is one we rarely see until it has already been paid.
